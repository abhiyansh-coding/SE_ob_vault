## Notes

  assumed. Structure: A 5×2 · B 5×6 · C 2×10 · D 2×10.
- The handout weights the End Term at **40 marks of 100**; this paper is out of
  80 and is scaled. The paper's own structure is what matters here, not the 80.
- **Marks are assigned below by the topic each question tests, not by its
  printed CO** — and here the two mostly agree, which they did not in the CN
  corpus. The one disagreement is noted in the table.

  ("a company is using…", "a health-tech startup plans to…") and ask for a

## Judgment calls

Three classifications are arguable and are recorded rather than hidden:

- **B1 case 2** ("working system using reusable components", users give feedback,
  iterations continue) reads as either **RAD** (conventional, lectures 6-7) or
  **Component-Based Development** (evolutionary, lecture 8). "Reusable
  components" is the stronger signal, so its 2 marks sit with
  [[Evolutionary Process Models]]. If a solution key says RAD, move it.
- **B3** is tagged **CO1** on the paper, which maps to lectures 1-12, so it is
  filed under [[SDLC & CMMI]] (lecture 12). The content — effort distribution,
  the 40-20-40 rule — is equally at home in [[Effort Estimation & COCOMO]].
  This is the one place the printed CO and the natural topic disagree.
- **A5** was first filed under [[Effort Estimation & COCOMO]] on topical grounds
  (the effort/time relationship) and **moved to
  [[Introduction to Software Engineering]] on 2026-09-02** after the lecture 1-8
  deck was read. The deck teaches this exact point in its *software crisis*
  slide, the paper tags the question **CO1** (lectures 1-12), and lecture 17 is
  CO2. Deck evidence and the printed CO agreed against the first guess.
- **C1** is one integrated basis-path exercise spanning two lectures. The CFG
  and V(G) parts (6) sit with [[Cyclomatic Complexity & Graph Matrices]]
  (lecture 39); the independent-path enumeration (4) with
  [[White-Box Testing]] (lecture 38).
